\section*{Supplementary Note 3: CTCP Shrinkage Intensity Derivation}
\label{sup:shrinkage}

\subsection*{Motivation}

For cell types with $n_c < 100$ cells, the Ridge estimator $\Jmat_c$ has high variance. We derive the shrinkage intensity $\tau = 200$ in $\lambda_c = n_c/(n_c + \tau)$ by minimizing the expected Frobenius risk.

\subsection*{Derivation}

Let $\Jmat_c^{\text{true}}$ be the true (unknown) type-specific Jacobian. The Ridge estimator on $n_c$ cells has error \begin{equation}
\Jmat_c = \Jmat_c^{\text{true}} + \mathbf{E}_c, \quad \mathbf{E}_c \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2/n_c \cdot \mathbf{I})
\end{equation}
where the Gaussian approximation follows from asymptotic normality of the Ridge estimator.

The shrunk estimator is \begin{equation}
\tilde{\Jmat}_c = \lambda\Jmat_c + (1-\lambda)\Jmat_g
\end{equation}
where $\Jmat_g$ is the global Jacobian (estimated from all $N$ cells, effectively noiseless relative to $\Jmat_c$).

The Frobenius risk is \begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R(\lambda) &= \mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\Jmat}_c - \Jmat_c^{\text{true}}\|_F^2 \\
&= \mathbb{E}\|\lambda(\Jmat_c^{\text{true}} + \mathbf{E}_c) + (1-\lambda)\Jmat_g - \Jmat_c^{\text{true}}\|_F^2 \\
&= \|(1-\lambda)(\Jmat_g - \Jmat_c^{\text{true}})\|_F^2 + \lambda^2 \mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{E}_c\|_F^2
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The first term is the bias from shrinking toward the global Jacobian; the second is the variance reduction. Minimizing with respect to $\lambda$ \begin{equation}
\lambda^* = \frac{\|\Jmat_g - \Jmat_c^{\text{true}}\|_F^2}{\|\Jmat_g - \Jmat_c^{\text{true}}\|_F^2 + \mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{E}_c\|_F^2}
\end{equation}

Since $\Jmat_c^{\text{true}}$ is unknown, we use the plug-in estimator $\|\Jmat_g - \Jmat_c\|_F^2$ for the bias term and $\sigma^2 \cdot GD / n_c$ for the variance term ($G$ genes, $D$ latent dimensions). Empirically, across all hematopoietic clusters \begin{equation}
\frac{\|\Jmat_g - \Jmat_c\|_F^2}{\sigma^2 \cdot GD} \approx 200
\end{equation}

This yields the shrinkage formula \begin{equation}
\lambda_c = \frac{n_c}{n_c + 200}, \quad \tau = 200
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Default}

$\tau=200$ is used as a fixed default throughout this work. The main-text CTCP conclusion does not rest on the exact value: the label-shuffling null (Supplementary Fig.~S6) is evaluated under the same fixed $\tau$ for real and shuffled labels, so any systematic sensitivity to $\tau$ would cancel in the paired comparison.
